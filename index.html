Quick Start

  1. Load Audio: Click the 1. Upload Sample button to choose an audio file from your computer. Once processed, its visual profile will render in the central waveform display.
  2. Set Output Level: Move the Volume slider up. Note: The synthesizer defaults to 0% volume to ensure your hardware or hearing isn't startled by a loud initial signal.
  3. Activate Engine: Click the 2. ▶ Start button. The text will change to Stop, and the granular audio engine will begin firing.
  4. Navigate: Click the waveform display with your cursor to move the silver positioning bar. This shifts the exact spot in the file where grains are being captured.

Grain Morphology

This module dictates the structural geometry and harvesting location of individual grains.

  • Grain Size (ms): Controls the raw duration of each grain (from 100ms to 3000ms). Tiny settings yield pointillistic, ticking sounds; long settings yield recognizable loops.
  • Random Grain Size (%): Scales how much variance is applied to grain durations. At 0%, every grain is uniform. At higher values, grain sizes drift randomly for a less mechanical feel.
  • Overlap: Adjusts the density of the engine. Higher values pack more grains into the timeline simultaneously, blending them into a smooth, seamless background texture. Lower values cause gaps, introducing stutter patterns.
  • Position (%): The underlying timeline marker indicating where grains are pulled from the master sample. Changing this is mirrored instantly by the vertical playhead line.
  • Position Random (%): Creates spatial scattering around your selected Position. Keeping this at 0% freezes grain sampling to an exact millisecond. Raising it instructs the engine to scatter its grain selections forward or backward around that position, creating a blurred, abstract smear.

Motion & Dynamics

This module regulates velocity profiles, tracking speeds, and overall acoustic output.

  • Base Rate: Regulates the playback rate (and consequently pitch) of the grain stream. 1.00 preserves native speed/pitch. 2.00 forces a double-speed playback (up an octave), while 0.50 cuts speed in half (down an octave).
  • Random Rate: An advanced parameter accepting an array of numeric factors separated by commas (e.g., 1,1, or custom lists like 1, 0.5, 1.5). The engine jumps between these values randomly as new grains are spawned, making it easy to create complex arpeggiations or multi-octave textures.
  • Volume (%): Modulates the final master gain node before sending audio to your destination outputs.

Semitone Ratio Calculator

Because the Base Rate parameters require absolute playback speed multipliers instead of standard western note names, a conversion calculator is included at the bottom. It processes the underlying formula:

Ratio = 2^(Semitone_Offset / 12)

Type any integer value into the Semitone Offset input to see the exact numeric multiplier required to hit that musical note interval. You can type this result directly into the Base Rate control box to tune your synthesizer perfectly to scale.

Creative Techniques to Explore

  • The Time-Stretching Ambient Wash: Upload a melodic sample, increase Overlap to a high value, set Position Random to around 5%–15%, and slowly scrub through the waveform. This detaches the pitch from standard playback speed, freezing moments in time into massive, infinite drone pads.
  • Micro-Glitch Sequences: Pull your Grain Size down near its minimum (100ms) and lower the Overlap control. Turn up Random Grain Size slightly to generate crisp, rhythmic pops and skittering microsound rhythms.
  • Generative Harmonic Clusters: Use the Random Rate input array to play chords from a single sample tone. By inputting comma-separated ratio values like 0.5, 1, 1.259921, 1.498307, 2, the synthesizer will scatter its grains across multiple octave and chord intervals simultaneously.
